How It Began

From a six-table
eaterie to a family name

Our journey began in Dhanbad with a simple vision — to bring the soulful taste of Jharkhand to your plate. Founded on generations of recipes passed down through our family, we serve the food we grew up eating.

In 2014, Birsa Lakra turned his mother's kitchen into a six-table eatery on Bank More. He had one rule: every recipe must taste exactly the way his mother cooked it. No shortcuts, no compromises.

From Rugda foraged in the monsoon forests to the Pitha rolled on festive mornings, every dish carries a story. We source from local Santhali and Munda farmers, grind our own masalas, and cook in mustard oil the way our grandmother insisted.

What started as a six-table eatery on Bank More has grown into a family restaurant on City Center Mall's first floor — but the recipes, and the people who make them, haven't changed.

What Sets Us Apart

The Jai Johar difference

Four things we will never compromise on, no matter how busy the kitchen gets.

Forest-Foraged

Our Rugda mushrooms and bamboo shoots are foraged from Jharkhand's forests by Santhali gatherers — never farmed, never frozen.

Hand-Ground Masalas

Every spice is dry-roasted and stone-ground in our kitchen each morning. No pre-packaged powders, ever.

Family Recipes

Every recipe on our menu comes from the Lakra family kitchen — three generations of tribal cooking, tested by time.

Our Journey

Ten years, one vision

The milestones that shaped Jai Johar.

2014

The First Table

Birsa Lakra opens a six-table eatery on Bank More, Dhanbad, with his mother's recipes and a single gas stove.

2017

The Rugda Year

Our Rugda curry wins the Dhanbad Food Festival's Best Tribal Dish award. The queue begins.

2020

Bigger Home

We move to the first floor of City Center Mall — a proper dining room, but the same kitchen and the same cooks.

2023

The Bamboo Kitchen

We add a live bamboo-cooking station — guests can watch bamboo chicken being prepared the tribal way.

2026

25,000 Families

We cross 25,000 families served. The thali hasn't changed. Neither has the family behind it.
